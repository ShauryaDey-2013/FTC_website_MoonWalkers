# 📦 Blog Log #1: Unboxing the goBILDA Starter Kit & Assembling Our First FTC Chassis

The leap from plastic LEGO pegs to heavy-duty aluminum channels is officially underway! This week, Team [XXXX] received our **goBILDA Strafer Chassis Kit**, marking our first official build session as a FIRST Tech Challenge rookie team.

Transitioning from FLL means we are leaving block-snapping behind and picking up hex keys, locknuts, and precision metal gears. Here is how our first build day went down.

---

## 📦 The Unboxing: A Whole New World of Hardware

When the massive goBILDA box arrived, the entire team gathered around. Coming from FLL, we are used to organized sorting trays of plastic beams. Opening this kit felt like stepping into a real mechanical machine shop.

* **First Impressions:** The build quality is incredible. Everything is heavy, anodized aluminum, and uses a pattern-based system (the 8mm grid) that makes alignment straightforward.
* **The Mecanum Wheels:** The biggest highlight for the team was holding the massive Mecanum wheels. Seeing the specialized, angled rollers in person made us realize just how agile an FTC robot can be compared to our old dual-wheel FLL robots.
* **The Hardware Inventory:** We spent the first hour sorting the different lengths of M4 screws, specialized bearing pillars, and the powerful Yellow Jacket planetary gearmotors.

## 🛠️ The Build Process: Assembling the Starter Robot

We decided to build the standard Strafer Chassis configuration to get a baseline understanding of how the goBILDA ecosystem fits together. We divided into two sub-teams: the Chassis Frame Team and the Drivetrain Team.

### Step 1: Squaring the Frame
Using the low-profile U-channels, we mapped out our outer square frame.
* **What we learned:** Tightening screws in metal is completely different than FLL. We quickly learned about "hand-tightening" everything first to ensure the frame stays perfectly square before locking down the screws with our hex drivers.

### Step 2: Mounting the Yellow Jacket Motors
Each of the four Mecanum wheels is powered by its own dedicated motor. We mounted the motors inside the U-channels to keep them protected from potential field collisions.
* **The Pivot:** We initially mounted one of the motor faceplates backward, which threw off the alignment of the drive axle. We had to disassemble the corner, consult the goBILDA digital blueprint, and re-mount it. A classic rookie mistake, but a great learning moment for our build team!

### Step 3: Installing the Mecanum Wheels
This was the most critical step. Mecanum wheels must be installed in a specific "X" pattern when looking at the robot from above. If you mix up the left and right wheels, the robot won't strafe correctly. We carefully checked the roller orientation before locking the hub collars into place.
